\title{CANONCITE: A Multilingual, Multi-Tradition Benchmark for\\
Canonical-Citation Attribution and Abstention}

\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{abstract}
When a language model cites a canonical source---a scripture verse, a
constitutional article---citing the \emph{wrong} unit is a distinct and more
damaging failure than citing nothing. Existing attribution benchmarks score
whether a claim is supported by a retrieved passage; they cannot detect that the
model named \textsc{Gita} 2.48 when the source was 2.47. We introduce
\textbf{CANONCITE}, a benchmark for \emph{exact-ID} citation attribution and
abstention over ten public-domain canonical corpora spanning four traditions and
five scripts (188{,}557 citable units), with every question posed in English,
Hindi, and the corpus's native script. Instantiating a five-system ladder, we
show that the cross-lingual attribution collapse is fundamentally a
\emph{retrieval-ranking} failure: on our pilot corpora, attribution F1 under
non-English queries rises from 0.177 to 0.690 through hybrid and reranked
retrieval alone, with the gold unit retrieved but ranked at median 7--13
throughout. We then show that the residual errors
are dominated by \emph{near misses}---an adjacent verse on the same theme---which
neither a topical reranker nor a per-passage verifier resolves, and that a joint
discriminative exact-ID selector cuts misattribution by 29--61\% at
$\sim$2 LLM calls per item versus the Self-RAG/CRAG baseline's $\sim$10. A
three-point reader study (8B/14B/120B) bounds this claim honestly: the advantage
requires a sufficiently capable reader, below which decomposed per-passage
reflection wins, and above which our margin \emph{widens monotonically with
scale}. We release the corpora, items, metric harness, and all system code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation is now the default remedy for factual
hallucination, and a mature literature evaluates whether a generated claim is
\emph{supported} by the passages a system retrieved
\cite{gao2023alce,bohnet2022aqa,hu2025caqa}. That framing has a blind spot. In
domains organised around a fixed, publicly known identifier scheme---scripture
verses, statutes, constitutional articles, case law---the citation is not a
pointer to evidence but a \emph{claim about identity}. A model that answers a
question about non-attachment correctly but attributes it to
Bhagavad G\={\i}t\={a} 2.48 instead of 2.47 has produced a fluent,
well-supported, and false citation. Support-based metrics score it as a success:
the neighbouring verse is on the same theme and entails much the same content.

This failure is most consequential exactly where canonical texts matter most and
where NLP resources are thinnest: religious and legal corpora in Indian
languages, read by users querying in Hindi or in the text's own script. Yet
Indic LLM benchmarks evaluate \emph{understanding} through multiple-choice
questions \cite{verma2024milu,parambench2025,indicqa2024}, and the one prior
line that exploits fixed identifiers for exact attribution does so for a single
jurisdiction in a single language \cite{ovcharov2026citation}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We are careful about novelty. We do \emph{not} claim
the fixed-ID-enables-exact-attribution idea, already demonstrated for one legal
jurisdiction \cite{ovcharov2026citation}, nor first religious verse-attribution,
already done for a single tradition \cite{islamiceval2025}, nor Indian scripture
knowledge \cite{parambench2025,verma2024milu}. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{C1 --- Benchmark.} The first multilingual, multi-tradition,
multi-script canonical-citation attribution and abstention benchmark: ten
corpora, 188{,}557 citable units, 622 items, each posed in English, Hindi, and
native script. The \emph{cross-lingual, multi-script attribution axis} is the
load-bearing, genuinely unoccupied novelty (\S\ref{sec:bench}).
\item \textbf{C2 --- Metric suite.} A corpus-agnostic exact-attribution harness
operating over normalised IDs, integrating the fixed-ID existence check
\cite{ovcharov2026citation} with the citation precision/recall paradigm
\cite{gao2023alce} and adding misattribution and abstention measures
(\S\ref{sec:metrics}).
\item \textbf{C3 --- Method.} A joint discriminative exact-ID selector (E2) that
resolves near-miss errors a topical reranker and a binary verifier structurally
cannot, at roughly a fifth of the SOTA's inference cost---holding above a reader
capacity threshold we locate between 8B and 14B (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{C4 --- Findings.} The cross-lingual collapse is localised to
retrieval \emph{ranking}; residual errors are dominated by near misses; and
MAR-exist $=0.000$ throughout separates ``cannot read the language'' from
``misattributes''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Status of this release.} CANONCITE v1 ships model-drafted items whose
gold citations are sampled from, and automatically validated against, the closed
corpus ID space. Human verification is instrumented and \emph{in progress on a
stratified sample}; we report its coverage and agreement in
\S\ref{sec:agreement} and state plainly what it does not yet cover. All system
comparisons are \emph{relative} comparisons over a common, automatically
validated label set: the caveat applies identically to every system, so the
orderings are unaffected, while absolute values may shift.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Attribution benchmarks.} ALCE \cite{gao2023alce}, AQA
\cite{bohnet2022aqa}, and CAQA \cite{hu2025caqa} score whether a generated claim
is entailed by retrieved passages, typically via NLI, in English. Their unit of
truth is \emph{support}, not identity; a near-miss citation to a semantically
equivalent neighbour is scored correct. Surveys of evidence-based generation
\cite{evidencesurvey2025} confirm this framing dominates.

\paragraph{Fixed-ID attribution.} \citet{ovcharov2026citation} shows that a
closed citation graph turns verification into an exact lookup, for US legal
citations in English. We adopt the idea and credit it; our contribution is
extending it across traditions, scripts, and query languages, and specifying its
validation against human judgement---a generality claim no prior fixed-ID work
makes.

\paragraph{Indic and religious NLP.} MILU \cite{verma2024milu}, ParamBench
\cite{parambench2025} and IndicQA \cite{indicqa2024} evaluate Indic
\emph{understanding} via MCQ; IL-TUR \cite{iltur2024} and ILDC
\cite{malik2021ildc} cover Indian legal reasoning without exact-citation
scoring. IslamicEval \cite{islamiceval2025} targets verse hallucination in one
tradition, monolingually. None measures exact-ID attribution across languages
and scripts.

\paragraph{Repair methods.} CiteFix \cite{maheshwari2025citefix}, VeriCite
\cite{qian2025vericite}, RARR \cite{gao2023rarr}, Self-RAG
\cite{asai2024selfrag} and CRAG \cite{yan2024crag} all verify or repair
per-passage. \S\ref{sec:results} shows this family is \emph{subsumed} once
ranking is fixed, and that joint discrimination is what the residual errors
require.

\section{The CANONCITE Benchmark}
\label{sec:bench}

\paragraph{Corpora.} Ten public-domain canonical texts spanning Hindu, Buddhist,
Sikh and Christian traditions plus Indian constitutional law, in five scripts
(Table~\ref{tab:corpora}). Each corpus is frozen as a
\texttt{corpus\_index.jsonl} keyed by its own canonical identifier scheme
(\texttt{2.47} for G\={\i}t\={a} chapter.verse; \texttt{Art.~370} for the
Constitution), giving a closed ID space $U$ per corpus. Only public-domain
translations are released; where the only English translation is under copyright
(R\={a}m\={a}ya\d{n}a, Mah\={a}bh\={a}rata, Guru Granth Sahib), we release the
native-script text alone---an honest constraint that makes those corpora
\emph{require} cross-lingual retrieval.

\paragraph{Items.} 622 items across all ten corpora, each carrying a question, a
gold answer, gold citation ID(s) validated against $U$, near-miss distractors
(adjacent same-theme units), a question type, and an ambiguity label. Five
question types---\emph{factual}, \emph{retrieval}, \emph{conceptual},
\emph{interpretive}, \emph{unanswerable}---exercise different failure modes;
unanswerable items carry \texttt{must\_abstain} and no gold citation, so
abstention is measured rather than assumed. Every item is fully trilingual:
Hindi and native-script questions were produced with IndicTrans2
\cite{gala2023indictrans2} (P\={a}li by prompted generation, which IndicTrans2
does not cover). Gold citations are language-independent, so the three language
conditions are matched by construction---the comparison isolates query language
and nothing else.

\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

All metrics operate over normalised IDs against the closed space $U$, so
verification is exact lookup with no NLI model. Let an item have gold set $G$ and
a system emit cited set $C$.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Attribution P/R/F1 (exact)}: set overlap of $C$ and $G$. We also
report a \emph{span} variant crediting a correct chapter with a wrong verse.
\item \textbf{Misattribution Rate (MAR)}: the fraction of \emph{citing} items
where $C \not\subseteq G$---the benchmark's gated axis. It decomposes into
\textbf{MAR-exist} (cited an ID not in $U$, i.e.\ a fabricated identifier) and
\textbf{MAR-support} (cited a real but wrong unit).
\item \textbf{Near-miss MAR (NMR)}: MAR restricted to citations landing on a
declared near-miss distractor.
\item \textbf{Abstention accuracy}, \textbf{over-citation} and
\textbf{wrong-abstention} rates, over the \texttt{must\_abstain} items.
\end{itemize}

MAR-exist is 0.000 in every run we report: models cite \emph{real} units
incorrectly rather than inventing identifiers. This separates a base-competence
failure from an attribution failure and is why exact-ID scoring is informative
here.

\section{Systems and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The ladder.} \textbf{A} naive BM25 RAG; \textbf{B} hybrid BM25+dense
RRF (BGE-M3 \cite{chen2024bgem3} + FAISS); \textbf{C} hybrid plus cross-encoder
reranking; \textbf{D} an inference-time reproduction of Self-RAG
\cite{asai2024selfrag} + CRAG \cite{yan2024crag}, where a CRAG-style evaluator
labels each passage and a Self-RAG ISSUP critique keeps a citation only if the
passage supports it; and \textbf{E2} (ours), which presents all reranked
candidates \emph{jointly} and forces one exact-source choice or abstention---one
LLM call rather than up to $k$. Grid: systems $\times$ 10 corpora $\times$ query
language (en/hi/native) $=$ 28 cells per system. Primary reader
Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct \cite{qwen2024}, self-hosted; all prompts, temperatures and
$k$ fixed and logged. We pre-committed to a decision gate: \emph{E2 must beat D
on cross-lingual MAR}.

\paragraph{The collapse is a ranking failure.} On the two pilot corpora System A
attains 0.719 attribution F1 under English queries and collapses to 0.177 under
Hindi/native queries, with misattribution at 0.757. A recall probe resolves the
cause: the gold unit \emph{is} retrieved under cross-lingual queries but ranked
at median 7--13, outside the reader's window. Fixing ranking alone---dense
hybrid retrieval, then cross-encoder reranking---lifts cross-lingual F1 to 0.626
and then 0.690 and cuts MAR from 0.757 to 0.196 (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}),
without touching the reader. Notably C@5 (0.690) beats E@10 (0.659): reranking
beats simply widening $k$. Applying the binary verify-and-repair layer (E) to
BM25 retrieval barely moves cross-lingual F1 (0.177 $\to$ 0.173): a verifier can
only repair \emph{to} a unit retrieval actually surfaced, so this family is
subsumed once ranking is fixed.

\paragraph{The residual errors are near misses, and joint selection resolves
them.} After reranking, the dominant remaining error is a citation to an
adjacent unit on the same theme---precisely what a \emph{topical} reranker
cannot separate, since the neighbour is topically as good, and what a
\emph{binary per-passage} verifier cannot separate either, since the neighbour
independently looks supportive. E2's joint presentation makes that comparison
the model's actual task. Across all ten corpora it attains the lowest
cross-lingual MAR of any system (0.387 vs D's 0.443; Table~\ref{tab:gate}),
winning 7 of 10 corpora, with the largest margins where near-miss dominates
(Upani\d{s}ads $0.427\!\to\!0.125$; Mah\={a}bh\={a}rata
$0.472\!\to\!0.312$). \textbf{The pre-committed gate passes.} Its three losses
are instructive rather than random: Guru Granth Sahib, R\={a}m\={a}ya\d{n}a and
Thirukku\d{r}a\d{l} are the low-resource-script corpora, where every system
sits at MAR $>0.2$ and two exceed $0.8$---once the reader cannot read the
script at all, there is no discrimination left for a selector to make.

\paragraph{The gate is reader-capacity-dependent.} Re-running C, E2 and D on all
28 cells with Aya-Expanse-8B \cite{aya2024expanse} \emph{flips} the result: D
attains 0.577 cross-lingual MAR against E2's 0.604. The mechanism is visible in
intervention behaviour---under the weaker reader E2's single joint call becomes
erratic (499 repairs, 26.4\% abstention, vs D's 176 and 16.7\%). A one-call
discriminative select requires the capacity to hold eight candidates and an
exact-source criterion simultaneously; D decomposes the same work into
individually easy judgements an 8B model can still execute.

\paragraph{Above the threshold, our margin widens with scale.} We therefore added
a third capacity point, gpt-oss-120B, on the pilot corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity}). E2's margin over D grows monotonically---0.013 (8B),
0.027 (14B), 0.081 (120B)---and its cut in misattribution relative to the
reranking baseline strengthens in step: $-29\%$, $-40\%$, $-61\%$. At 120B, E2
attains the lowest cross-lingual MAR we measure for any system--reader pair
(0.121) and wins all four cross-lingual cells.

\paragraph{Scope limit.} The 120B column covers two corpora, not ten, and is
\emph{not} interchangeable with the 28-cell result. On this pilot subset the
gate holds for all three readers---including 8B, which flips only on the full
grid---so these cells do not themselves discriminate by capacity. What
Table~\ref{tab:capacity} establishes is that at the top of the range the margin
is the widest observed, in the direction the capacity mechanism predicts.
Confirming it survives the seven harder corpora requires the all-ten grid at
this capacity and remains open.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} C3 lands as a \emph{capacity-matched selection
rule}: one cheap joint call when the reader can carry it, decomposed
per-passage verification when it cannot, with the crossover between 8B and 14B.
Because frontier-scale readers are the deployment target for citation-critical
systems, the regime in which E2 is preferred is the one that matters in
practice---and it is the cheaper one.

\paragraph{Closed-book control.} The RAG numbers above are only meaningful
against a baseline of what the reader already knows. These are famous
public-domain texts, so parametric recall is a live confound; and a reader that
cannot parse a Tamil or Gurmukhi question will fail whatever we retrieve. We
therefore run the same items with \emph{no retrieval at all}: the question is
asked directly and the model must cite an ID from the closed space or abstain.
\textbf{[Table and figures inserted on completion of the run.]} This control
also makes MAR-exist interpretable: with nothing retrieved, any cited ID came
from memory alone.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All retrieval is local and deterministic given the
frozen corpora; dense caches are rebuilt from \texttt{corpus\_index.jsonl} with
BGE-M3. Reader temperature is fixed at 0.2 for RAG systems and 0.0 for the
closed-book control; prompts and $k$ are logged per run. Every cell is
checkpointed to JSONL, and every number in this paper is read verbatim from
those files by the harness rather than transcribed. The metric suite ships with
45 unit tests.

\section{Human Verification}
\label{sec:agreement}

Items are model-drafted with gold citations sampled from and validated against
$U$. To measure how far that automatic validation tracks human judgement, we
draw a deterministic stratified sample for double annotation: 120 items,
allocated proportionally across the seven corpora readable by our annotators
(English/Hindi/Devan\={a}gar\={\i}), stratified by question type, with floors on
unanswerable (20) and near-miss-bearing (105) items. Thirukku\d{r}a\d{l}
(Tamil), Guru Granth Sahib (Gurmukhi) and Dhammapada (P\={a}li) are excluded for
want of a script-competent annotator---a stated limitation, not a silent
omission. Two annotators independently approve, reject, or edit each item;
agreement is computed with Krippendorff's $\alpha$-MASI \cite{krippendorff2011,
passonneau2006masi} over set-valued citations, and agreeing verdicts are folded
into a gold set. \textbf{[Agreement figures and coverage inserted on

\section{Limitations and Ethics}

Items are model-drafted; human verification covers a stratified sample rather
than the full 622, so absolute values may shift even though system orderings are
unaffected. Three corpora lack script-competent annotation. The 120B reader
column covers two corpora. Hindi and native-script questions are machine
translated, so translation artefacts are a confound we bound but do not
eliminate. All corpora are public domain and we release only public-domain
translations; for the three copyright-constrained corpora we release
native-script text alone. The benchmark measures \emph{attribution, not
endorsement}: citing a verse correctly is a factual claim about provenance and
carries no theological position. We follow the datasheet convention of
\citet{gebru2021datasheets}.

\section{Conclusion}

Citing the wrong canonical unit is a distinct failure that support-based
attribution metrics cannot see, and it is worst exactly where canonical texts
carry most weight and NLP resources are thinnest. CANONCITE measures it directly
over ten corpora, five scripts, and three query-language conditions. Our results
localise the cross-lingual collapse to retrieval ranking, show the residual
errors are near misses that per-passage verification structurally cannot
resolve, and establish that joint discriminative exact-ID selection resolves
them at a fifth of the SOTA's cost---above a reader-capacity threshold we
locate, and with a margin that widens as readers scale. We release the corpora,
items, harness, and all system code.
